# CoAuthorLab — Reframed Product & Brand Brief

**Working tagline:** *Where serious student research begins.*  
**Launch focus:** India-first, global-ready  
**Product type:** Research guidance, mentorship and scholarly portfolio platform

---

## 1. The refined idea

CoAuthorLab is a professional research network for students who are ready to move beyond a vague project idea but do not yet have the structure, resources or guidance to do meaningful research.

It helps a student turn an early thought into a credible research path: understand the field, study trustworthy sources, choose a focused question, plan the work, receive mentor feedback, and present their progress or published outcomes in a professional profile.

This is not an AI chat website, a paper dump, or a service that writes research for students. It is a calm, credible workspace where **AI provides structure and source-aware assistance, while people provide experience, review and accountability.**

### The product promise

> **Bring an idea. Leave with a research direction, a practical next step, and the right people around you.**

### The research journey

```text
Idea → Direction → Evidence → Plan → Mentor feedback → Build → Document → Publish / Present
```

---

## 2. Positioning

### One-sentence positioning

> **CoAuthorLab helps emerging researchers turn ideas into well-guided, evidence-aware work through AI, trusted resources and verified mentors.**

### What distinguishes it

| Typical tool | CoAuthorLab |
| --- | --- |
| Gives a long list of papers | Explains what to read first and why it matters |
| Offers a generic AI conversation | Guides a clear research workflow with cited sources |
| Treats mentors as an afterthought | Makes mentor discovery, requests and feedback part of the journey |
| Hides student work in a dashboard | Lets students create a controlled, professional research portfolio |
| Focuses only on publication | Supports projects, prototypes, posters, articles, papers and presentations |

### Tone

Thoughtful, precise, encouraging and academically grounded. The platform should make a beginner feel supported without speaking down to them, and make a mentor feel that their reputation and time are respected.

---

## 3. Core user roles

### Student / emerging researcher

Explores an idea, builds a private workspace, discovers credible resources, asks for AI guidance, works with a mentor and chooses what—if anything—to publish publicly.

### Mentor / guide

A professor, PhD candidate, researcher or experienced industry practitioner who can publish a profile, define expertise and availability, receive requests, offer sessions or review work.

### Contributor

A student, mentor, lab or organisation that may share a research article, project update, paper, poster, thesis summary, prototype, event or opportunity.

### Institution / lab (future)

Shares research groups, opportunities, calls for students, workshops and verified faculty or lab profiles.

## 4. Pillar one — Guided research, not generic AI

The AI experience should be task-based, source-aware and quiet in the interface. It should never dominate the product with an oversized chat box or exaggerated claims.

Key guided actions:

- Shape an idea into a research direction.
- Explain a paper, method or concept in the learner’s context.
- Build a reading path from reliable source records.
- Compare papers and surface recurring limitations as **possible directions**, never confirmed novelty.
- Create a project, experiment or proposal plan.
- Prepare questions for a mentor meeting.
- Help organise notes and references.

Every source-backed response must show the underlying source. Every generated suggestion must be visibly labelled as an AI suggestion. The system must refuse fabricated citations, plagiarism requests, fabricated results and claims of guaranteed publication or patentability.

---

## 5. Pillar two — Mentor network

Mentorship is a headline product capability, not simply a directory link.

### Add a mentor

The platform needs an **“Become a Mentor”** entry point in the public navigation and a dedicated onboarding flow.

Mentor onboarding collects:

- Name, photo, role and short professional introduction
- Institution, company or independent practice
- Areas of expertise and research keywords
- Degrees, experience, language and time zone
- Public professional links (ORCID, Google Scholar, LinkedIn, personal site or institutional profile)
- Publication highlights, patents, talks, projects or awards
- Mentoring formats: written feedback, one-off session, recurring guidance, proposal review, workshop
- Price or free/volunteer availability, calendar and response expectations
- Verification documents and consent

### Mentor profile

A mentor profile should feel like a restrained academic profile—not a gig marketplace. It includes verified credentials, expertise, selected work, availability, ratings/reviews where appropriate, and a clear “Request guidance” action.

### Trust and workflow

- Clearly distinguish email, identity, credential and research verification.
- Let students submit a short research context and specific request, rather than sending unrestricted direct messages.
- Keep request acceptance, scheduling, payment, feedback and moderation in the platform.
- Allow mentor notes to remain private by default.
- Never imply that verification guarantees advice quality or publication outcomes.

## 6. Pillar three — Publications, articles and research posts

Create a structured area called **Research Journal** (or **Research Showcase**). It is for sharing scholarly progress and useful work—not for becoming a noisy social feed.

### What users can publish

- Original research article or article summary
- Published paper record with DOI/link and permitted abstract/metadata
- Preprint or thesis summary
- Project case study or prototype update
- Poster, presentation or conference reflection
- Technical explainer / learning note
- Patent or IP update, without exposing confidential details
- Workshop, competition, internship or call for collaborators

### Important distinction

The platform must distinguish content types visibly:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Published work** | A record linked to an externally published article, paper, patent or proceedings page |
| **Preprint / manuscript** | Author-provided, not necessarily peer reviewed |
| **Project update** | Progress shared by its owner; not a research claim validation |
| **Expert article** | Educational or editorial content |
| **Opportunity** | Event, grant, internship, workshop or call for participation |

### Publishing workflow

1. Choose a post type.
2. Add title, authors/contributors, summary, tags, cover image and supporting links.
3. Declare ownership and confirm rights to every uploaded file and image.
4. Add DOI, repository link, publisher page or ORCID record where applicable.
5. Submit for automated checks and moderation when required.
6. Publish to the user’s profile and, where approved, the Research Journal.

### Rights, copyright and integrity

- Do not host copied publisher PDFs, copyrighted figures, paywalled article content or unlicensed images.
- Store only permitted metadata, short user-authored summaries and authorised uploads.
- Permit links to original publisher/repository pages.
- Require users to confirm content ownership or permission to publish.
- Provide report, takedown and edit/delete controls.
- Use AI only to help with outlining, accessibility text, summaries and discovery; never misrepresent AI-assisted text as peer-reviewed research.

## 8. Visual identity direction

CoAuthorLab must not look like the familiar “AI startup” template: glowing gradients, robot imagery, noisy glass cards, dark sci-fi panels or a giant chat interface.

Instead, it should feel like a contemporary independent research journal combined with a well-designed studio workspace.

### Design character

- Editorial, composed and confident
- Warm white and stone backgrounds rather than generic pure-white SaaS screens
- Deep ink/navy as the foundation, with one restrained mineral accent (cobalt, saffron or muted teal)
- High-quality serif display type for editorial moments; readable sans-serif for product UI
- Fine rules, generous space, purposeful typography and subtle paper-like texture
- Real interface previews, structured diagrams, source cards and mentor portraits—not decorative AI imagery

### Imagery

Use only:

- Original photography owned or licensed by CoAuthorLab
- Images with clear commercial licences and documented attribution where needed
- Commissioned illustrations
- AI-generated images created specifically for the product, with no imitation of living artists, brands or copyrighted characters

Avoid:

- Copyrighted images copied from search results
- Generic robot heads, circuit-board wallpaper, fake academic stock photos or excessive abstract blobs
- Images that suggest an untrue affiliation, research result or mentor credential

AI-generated imagery should be sparse and purposeful: quiet editorial illustrations, conceptual scientific forms or abstract spatial compositions. It should support a section, never become the brand’s entire personality.

### Domain-ready brand standard

Before purchasing and using a domain, check name, domain and trademark availability. Create a simple, distinct logo system and a compact brand guide so the website, email, social profiles and product UI use the same voice and visual language.

## 9. MVP scope

The first release should prove one outcome: a student can bring an idea, receive a credible next step, save sources and connect with an appropriate mentor.

### Include in MVP

- Distinctive public landing page and responsive design system
- Student onboarding and private research workspace
- Idea-to-direction guided flow
- Source-backed resource discovery and saved resources
- Basic AI research assistant with source/citation safeguards
- Mentor directory, mentor onboarding, verification review and guidance requests
- Public profiles with optional publication/project highlights
- Research Journal with structured post types, draft/review/publish states and reporting
- Admin moderation for mentors and public submissions
- Clear privacy, rights and research-integrity controls

### Defer until after validation

- Open direct messaging
- Advanced community feeds
- Full paper deep analysis and research-gap analysis
- In-platform mentor video calls and payouts
- Patent explorer
- Institution dashboards and collaboration marketplace

---

## 10. Final build brief

> **Build CoAuthorLab as a trusted, editorially designed research companion for emerging researchers. It should help students turn uncertain ideas into evidence-aware plans, discover useful sources, work privately, find verified mentors and share selected publications or project outcomes through a structured professional profile. AI should be visible as a useful capability, not treated as the product’s personality. The design must feel calm, original and domain-ready: more like a modern research publication and less like a conventional AI-generated SaaS website.**
